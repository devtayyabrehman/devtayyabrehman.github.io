WooCommerce Order Bumps: How They Work, Setup, and Cost

Understand checkout order bumps, choose a compatible WooCommerce extension, configure an offer, and verify its price and order data.

An order bump is an optional, related offer shown during checkout before the customer places the order. The shopper can add it without leaving the checkout flow. It differs from a cart cross-sell shown earlier and a post-purchase upsell shown after an order is submitted. WooCommerce extensions implement order-bump interfaces; the core store already supports product upsells and cross-sells in other contexts, but you should not assume it includes the exact checkout bump workflow you want.

This guide covers the decision and setup process without assuming one plugin, checkout template, payment gateway, or license plan. Treat prices and feature availability as vendor-specific: check the extension's current plan, renewal terms, supported checkout type, and compatibility before purchasing.

When does an order bump make sense?

An offer is useful when it genuinely complements the main purchase and is easy to understand quickly. Examples might include a protective case with a device or an installation service with equipment. The offer should have a clear price, fulfillment method, and effect on shipping or taxes. A discount is not required by definition; if you offer one, show the actual total and do not imply a saving that is not real.

Avoid presenting unrelated products or repeatedly interrupting the checkout. A bump should be optional, clearly labeled, and accessible using keyboard and screen-reader interaction. The checkout must continue to work if the shopper ignores it.

Is an order bump free or paid?

WooCommerce core does not provide every plugin's checkout order-bump feature as a standard built-in checkout control. You generally add it through an extension or a custom implementation. Some vendors may include limited functionality in a free edition, while advanced rules, checkout layouts, analytics, or support may require a paid plan. Which features are available changes over time.

Before installing, compare:

Question Why it matters
Does the free tier include the bump itself? A plugin page may advertise bumps while reserving them for a paid tier
Does it support your checkout type? Classic checkout and Checkout Block integrations can differ
Does it support your payment gateway? Payment flows and express buttons can bypass custom markup
Is the price annual, per site, or usage-based? Renewal and site limits affect ongoing cost
Does it support variable and subscription products? Product type changes pricing and fulfillment logic
How are taxes, shipping, and refunds handled? The bump becomes part of a real order

Do not evaluate cost only by the first-year promotion. Check renewal cost, support period, update access, compatibility policy, and whether the plugin depends on another premium extension. If a custom developer builds the feature, include maintenance and payment-flow testing in the estimate.

How do you configure an order bump safely?

The exact interface depends on the extension, but the decision sequence is similar:

  1. Install the extension on staging and confirm its documentation matches your WooCommerce checkout.
  2. Create or choose a complementary product with accurate title, price, tax, stock, and fulfillment settings.
  3. Configure the bump's trigger conditions, such as a specific product or category in the cart.
  4. Write concise copy that explains what the add-on is and what it costs.
  5. Set whether the offer is preselected. Prefer an explicit, informed choice; do not hide an opt-in or make the shopper hunt for how to decline.
  6. Test checkout with the offer selected and declined, including shipping, tax, coupons, and order total.
  7. Complete a test-mode payment and inspect the resulting order line items and email.

Test the extension's exact behavior around variable products, subscriptions, coupons, stock reduction, and refunds. A product being selected in an offer is not evidence that every payment method or checkout layout will carry it through correctly.

How should you choose the bump product?

Choose based on genuine relevance and operational fit, not only margin. A product that requires separate shipping can change delivery estimates. A virtual service may have different tax treatment. A subscription add-on can create recurring charges. Explain those consequences before the customer accepts.

Keep the offer's title and copy consistent with the product record. Avoid adding a product with hidden limitations or confusing variations. If there are multiple variants, make sure the checkout interface lets the shopper choose the correct one, and confirm that the selected variation—not merely its parent product—appears on the completed order.

The product picker may search by title, SKU, or another attribute depending on the extension. SKU is a useful inventory identifier, but do not assume every order-bump extension requires it to locate a product. The product's existence, published status, purchasability, stock state, variation configuration, and plugin rules are all worth checking if the offer is unavailable. The companion order-bump troubleshooting guide gives a step-by-step isolation process.

What should you test before going live?

Use a staging environment and payment gateway test mode. Place separate test orders with the bump accepted and declined. Verify the cart and checkout totals, tax lines, shipping, order confirmation, customer email, stock changes, and refund workflow. Test a guest checkout and a signed-in customer, and check mobile width, keyboard focus, and screen-reader labels.

Also test failure cases: a bump product goes out of stock between page load and order submission; a coupon changes the subtotal; the shopper changes the main cart; or the payment gateway returns an error. The offer should not produce an incorrect total or create an order that omits the customer's selected option. Check plugin logs and WooCommerce status tools for errors, but redact customer information before sharing logs.

If your store uses WooCommerce's Checkout Block, verify compatibility explicitly. Extensions built for classic checkout hooks may not render in a block checkout. Do not install a second checkout plugin as a guess; first reproduce the issue on staging with one controlled configuration.

How do you evaluate whether to keep the bump?

Track the offer in a privacy-conscious way and compare meaningful outcomes: how often it is shown, accepted, refunded, or causes checkout errors. An acceptance rate by itself does not show whether the offer improved customer experience or profitability. Include product margin, fulfillment cost, refund rate, support contacts, and any change to checkout completion. Avoid attributing all changes to the bump when campaigns, prices, or seasonality also changed.

Give the feature an owner and a review date. Remove expired offers, update copy when stock or shipping changes, and retest after checkout, payment, or WooCommerce updates. If the product is no longer relevant, disabling the offer is better than leaving a broken or misleading prompt.

What is the difference between a bump, cross-sell, and upsell?

The names are used inconsistently by marketing tools, so check where the offer appears and when the order changes:

Offer type Typical placement Customer action
Cross-sell Product or cart page Adds a related item before checkout
Checkout order bump During checkout, before payment Selects an optional add-on in the current flow
Post-purchase upsell After the initial order is accepted Accepts a follow-up offer, often through a specialized payment flow

WooCommerce product settings support upsell and cross-sell relationships, but those placements are not automatically an order-bump interface. A post-purchase offer is also not the same as adding an item before the original payment: it can involve separate authorization, gateway rules, and refund handling. The terminology in a plugin's dashboard may differ, so read how that extension changes the cart and order.

If you only need to show related products on a product page, a dedicated checkout extension may be unnecessary. If you need a one-click post-purchase offer, verify gateway support and how the plugin handles declined or expired payment authorizations. Choose the least complex mechanism that meets the customer and operational requirement.

How should the offer affect tax, shipping, and refunds?

Treat the bump as a real line item, not a visual checkbox. Confirm how the extension adds the product to cart totals, calculates tax, applies coupons, reduces inventory, and presents the item in order administration. If the add-on ships separately, clarify shipping costs and delivery timing before consent. For downloadable or service products, explain fulfillment and any activation steps.

Support staff should be able to identify the bump on the order and remove or refund it without guessing. Test partial refunds and full refunds using a staging gateway or the payment provider's documented test environment. Some payment methods settle differently; do not promise that an item can be removed after payment unless the extension and gateway support the operation.

Make the decline action visible and avoid pre-checking an offer in a way that could be confusing or non-compliant in the store's jurisdiction. Legal and payment requirements vary; if the offer changes recurring billing or subscription terms, get the appropriate review before launch.

Order bump launch checklist

  • Confirm the offer is relevant, optional, and clearly priced.
  • Verify licensing and compatibility for the exact checkout and gateway.
  • Test accept/decline flows, product types, shipping, tax, coupons, and refunds.
  • Inspect the final order and customer notifications.
  • Confirm keyboard access, mobile layout, and error recovery.
  • Monitor operational outcomes and remove stale offers.

Review WooCommerce's documentation for upsells and cross-sells and the Checkout Block. For a bump extension, use that vendor's current setup and pricing pages; extension costs and supported checkout integrations are not universal WooCommerce properties.
